Crisis Resources

If you or someone you love is struggling right now, you don't have to handle it alone. These free, confidential services are available any time, day or night.

In immediate danger? Call 911 now.

If you or someone else is at risk of harm right now, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. Don't wait for a call back from us.

Supporting others

If you're worried about someone

You don't need to be an expert to help. Showing up and staying calm can make a real difference.

Warning signs to take seriously

  • Talking about wanting to die, being a burden, or feeling trapped
  • Looking for ways to end their life
  • Withdrawing from friends, family or activities
  • Increased use of alcohol or drugs
  • Extreme mood swings, rage, or sudden calm after a hard period
  • Giving away belongings or saying goodbye
  • Sleeping much more or much less than usual

How to help

  • Ask directly. "Are you thinking about suicide?" Asking does not put the idea in someone's head.
  • Listen. Let them talk without judgment, and take what they say seriously.
  • Stay with them, or make sure someone else is with them, if they are at risk.
  • Remove access to means, such as firearms, medications or other ways they could harm themselves.
  • Connect them to help. Call or text 988 together. If they are in immediate danger, call 911.

Planning ahead

Making a safety plan

A safety plan is a short, personal list you write while you're feeling steady, so you have clear steps to follow if things get hard. Many people make one with their therapist.

  1. Know your warning signs

    Thoughts, feelings or situations that tell you a crisis may be building.

  2. Coping on your own

    Things that help you feel calmer, like a walk, music, a shower or breathing exercises.

  3. People and places

    People you can contact and places you can go to be around others or take your mind off things.

  4. Who to call for help

    Trusted people, your clinician, 988, and 911 in an emergency.

  5. Make your space safer

    Steps to limit access to firearms, medications or anything else you could use to harm yourself.

Keep your plan somewhere easy to find, like your phone or wallet, and share it with someone you trust. If you're a client, ask your clinician to build or review a plan with you.

When you're ready for ongoing care

Once you're safe, ongoing support can help you feel steadier over time. When it feels right, you can reach out to Clinic Ready Behavioral Health to talk about scheduling care. There's no rush. Your safety comes first.
