Insurance & Fees

Clear answers about cost, before your first visit.

Money questions shouldn't get in the way of care. Here's what we accept, what self-pay visits cost, and how to find out what you'll owe. Our billing team is happy to walk you through it at (555) 010-0123.

Check your coverage

How to verify your benefits

Call the member services number on the back of your insurance card, or log in to your plan's website. These questions will help you understand what you'll pay.

Questions to ask your insurer

  • Do I have outpatient mental health (behavioral health) benefits?
  • Is Clinic Ready Behavioral Health, or my specific clinician, in network?
  • Do I have a deductible? How much of it have I met this year?
  • What is my copay or coinsurance for therapy and psychiatry visits?
  • Do I need prior authorization or a referral before starting care?
  • Are telehealth visits covered the same as in-person visits?
  • Is there a limit on the number of sessions per year?

Have these ready when you call

  • Your member ID and group number
  • The policyholder's name and date of birth
  • The type of visit, such as "outpatient psychotherapy" or "psychiatric evaluation"
  • A pen to write down the date, the representative's name and a call reference number

Prefer not to make the call? Our care coordinators can check your benefits during your free 15-minute phone consult.

Ways to make care affordable

Options that can lower your cost

Sliding scale

A limited number of reduced-fee spots are available based on household income and size. Ask about sliding scale during your phone consult. We may ask for basic income information to confirm eligibility.

Out-of-network & superbills

If we're not in network with your plan, you can pay at the time of service and we'll give you a superbill: a detailed receipt with the codes your insurer needs. Many plans with out-of-network benefits reimburse part of the cost.

HSA & FSA

Mental health services are generally eligible expenses for Health Savings Accounts (HSA) and Flexible Spending Accounts (FSA). You can pay with your HSA or FSA card, or use your receipt to request reimbursement.

Your rights

Good Faith Estimate

You have the right to receive a "Good Faith Estimate" explaining how much your medical care will cost.

Under the No Surprises Act, health care providers must give patients who don't have insurance, or who are not using insurance, an estimate of the bill for items and services.

  • You have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate for the total expected cost of any non-emergency items or services, including related costs like tests or evaluations.
  • Make sure your provider gives you a Good Faith Estimate in writing at least 1 business day before your scheduled service. You can also ask for one before you schedule.
  • If you receive a bill that is at least $400 more than your Good Faith Estimate, you can dispute the bill.
  • Keep a copy or picture of your Good Faith Estimate.

How to request your estimate

Tell us when you schedule that you're uninsured or paying yourself, or call (555) 010-0123. We'll provide your estimate in writing, usually within [timeframe].

For questions or more information about your right to a Good Faith Estimate, visit www.cms.gov/nosurprises or call 1-800-985-3059.

Because therapy is ongoing, your estimate may cover an expected number of sessions over a period of time. We'll give you an updated estimate if your plan of care changes.

FAQ

Common billing questions

Will you check my insurance for me?

Yes. Share your insurance information when you request an appointment and a care coordinator will check your benefits before your first visit. We still recommend calling your insurer too, since they have the final word on coverage.

What if my plan isn't on your list?

Call us at (555) 010-0123. Our list changes, and some clinicians may be in network with plans the clinic isn't. If we're out of network, you can self-pay and use a superbill to seek reimbursement.

Is telehealth billed the same as in-person visits?

Our rates are the same for telehealth and in-person visits. Many insurance plans also cover telehealth the same way, but check with your plan to be sure.

Why did I get a bill after my visit?

If you haven't met your deductible, or your plan uses coinsurance, you may owe a balance after your insurer processes the claim. Your insurer will send an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) that shows what they paid and what you owe. Call us if anything looks wrong.

Who can get a Good Faith Estimate?

Anyone who is uninsured or who chooses not to use insurance for their care. Read more about your right to a Good Faith Estimate.

Will my insurance company know details of my therapy?

To process claims, insurers receive basic information such as dates of service, the type of service and a diagnosis code. They don't receive your therapy notes. Some people choose to self-pay for added privacy. See our Privacy & Notice of Practices for more.
