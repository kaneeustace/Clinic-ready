Other uses and disclosures, including most marketing and any sale of PHI, require your written authorization. You may revoke an authorization in writing at any time, except to the extent we have already relied on it.

Your rights

  • Access. You can ask to see or get an electronic or paper copy of your health record. We may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee.
  • Amendment. You can ask us to correct information you believe is incorrect or incomplete. We may say no, but we will tell you why in writing.
  • Accounting of disclosures. You can request a list of certain times we shared your information in the six years before your request.
  • Restrictions. You can ask us to limit what we use or share. We are not required to agree, except that if you pay for a service in full out of pocket, you can ask us not to share that information with your health plan, and we will agree unless the law requires us to share it.
  • Confidential communications. You can ask us to contact you in a specific way (for example, a particular phone number) or at a different address. We will agree to reasonable requests.
  • Paper copy of this notice. You can ask for a paper copy at any time, even if you agreed to receive it electronically.
  • Personal representative. If someone has medical power of attorney or is your legal guardian, they can exercise your rights.
  • File a complaint. If you believe your privacy rights have been violated, you can complain to our Privacy Officer (below) or to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office for Civil Rights by sending a letter to 200 Independence Avenue S.W., Washington, D.C. 20201, calling 1-877-696-6775, or visiting hhs.gov/ocr/complaints. We will not retaliate against you for filing a complaint.

Psychotherapy notes

Psychotherapy notes are a clinician's separate notes analyzing the content of a counseling session, kept apart from the rest of your record. They receive extra protection: in most cases we will not use or disclose them without your specific written authorization, and they are generally not part of the record available through the right of access. They do not include medication information, session times, test results, diagnoses or treatment plans, which are part of your regular record.

Substance use disorder records (42 CFR Part 2)

If you receive substance use disorder services from a program covered by federal confidentiality rules (42 CFR Part 2), those records have additional protections. Generally, they cannot be used or disclosed in civil, criminal, administrative or legislative proceedings against you without your written consent or a court order that meets Part 2 requirements. Telehealth privacy summary

Telehealth visits receive the same privacy protections as in-person care. Before your first video visit you will be asked to review and sign a telehealth informed consent. Key points:

  • Secure platform. We use a HIPAA-compliant video platform from a vendor that has signed a business associate agreement with us. [Name platform.] Sessions are not recorded by us without your written consent.
  • Your location. Clinicians generally must be licensed in the state where you are located at the time of the visit. We will confirm your location at the start of each session.
  • Your privacy at home. Please join from a private space, use a secure internet connection rather than public Wi-Fi, and avoid shared devices when possible.
  • Emergency plan. We will confirm a phone number and emergency contact so we can reach help if needed during a session.
  • Technology limits. No technology is risk-free. Connection problems may interrupt a session; if that happens, your clinician will call you at the number on file.
  • Your choice. You may choose in-person care instead, where available, at any time.
