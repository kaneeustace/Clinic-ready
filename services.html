Individual therapy

One-on-one support, at your pace

Therapy is a space to talk openly, understand what's going on and build skills that help in everyday life. Your therapist will tailor the approach to you, and you're always welcome to ask why we're doing something.

Approaches we use

CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy)

Notice the links between thoughts, feelings and actions, and practice new ways of responding. Often used for anxiety, depression, insomnia and OCD.

DBT (Dialectical Behavior Therapy)

Practical skills for handling intense emotions, distress and relationships, built around mindfulness and balancing acceptance with change.

EMDR

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing helps the brain process painful memories so they feel less overwhelming. Used mainly for trauma.

ACT (Acceptance & Commitment Therapy)

Make room for hard thoughts and feelings instead of fighting them, and take steps toward what matters most to you.

Psychodynamic therapy

Explore patterns from past experiences and relationships that may shape how you feel and act today, building deeper self-understanding.

Not sure which is right?

You don't need to pick. Many therapists blend approaches, and your clinician will recommend a plan after getting to know you.

What sessions are like

  • Sessions are typically 50 minutes, often weekly at first, then spaced out as you feel better.
  • Your first visit focuses on getting to know you, what brings you in and what you'd like to change.
  • Together you'll set goals and check in on progress regularly, so care stays useful.
  • Some approaches include practice between sessions, like trying a skill or keeping brief notes.
  • You can meet in person or by video, and switch between them if that works better.

Finding the right fit

Feeling comfortable with your therapist matters. If the match doesn't feel right, tell us. We'll help you switch clinicians, no explanation needed.

Psychiatry & medication

Thoughtful medication care, when it helps

Our psychiatric providers evaluate symptoms, talk through options and, if you choose, prescribe and monitor medication. You're part of every decision.

Psychiatric evaluation

A thorough first visit covering your history, current symptoms, health and goals. You'll leave with a clearer picture and a recommended plan, which may or may not include medication.

Medication management

Regular follow-ups to see how things are going, adjust doses, watch for side effects and answer questions. Visits are more frequent at the start and spaced out once things are stable.

Coordinated with therapy

With your permission, your psychiatric provider and therapist share updates so your care works as one plan. We can also coordinate with your primary care provider.

Couples & family therapy

Stronger connections, together

Relationships can be a big source of support, and of stress. Couples and family therapy gives everyone a fair, guided space to be heard.

Your therapist won't take sides. The goal is to understand each other's needs, break unhelpful patterns and find ways forward that work for the whole relationship or family.

Common reasons people come in

  • Communication breakdowns and recurring arguments
  • Rebuilding trust after a hard time
  • Parenting challenges and co-parenting
  • Big transitions: a new baby, a move, blended families, illness or loss
  • Supporting a family member with a mental health condition
  • Premarital or relationship check-ins

Sessions are typically 50–80 minutes [placeholder]. Some sessions may include individual check-ins with each person.

Teens & young adults (13–25)

Support for growing up in a complicated world

School, friendships, identity, social media, family and the move toward independence can all pile up. We offer care for ages 13–25 that takes young people seriously and meets them where they are.

What we help with

  • Anxiety & stress
  • Low mood
  • School pressure
  • Friendships & bullying
  • Identity & self-esteem
  • LGBTQ+ affirming support
  • ADHD
  • Self-harm
  • Family conflict
  • College & work transitions
  • Eating concerns
  • Substance use

Care may include individual therapy, family sessions, psychiatry and skills groups, depending on what fits.

Consent, privacy and parent involvement

  • For clients under 18, a parent or guardian usually needs to give consent to start care.
  • Teens get a private space to talk. What's shared in sessions generally stays between the teen and therapist.
  • We'll explain the limits of privacy up front, including when we must share information to keep someone safe.
  • Parents are often invited to check-ins or family sessions, with the teen's input on how that works.
  • Young adults 18 and over manage their own care and decide who, if anyone, is involved.

Telehealth

Video visits from wherever you're comfortable

Telehealth lets you meet with the same clinicians, for the same services, without the commute. Many people use a mix of in-person and video visits.

When a different level of care is a better fit

We provide outpatient care, which means scheduled visits while you continue living at home. Sometimes a person needs more support than weekly visits can offer, such as inpatient hospital care, residential treatment, intensive outpatient or partial hospitalization programs, or medically supervised detox.

If that's the case, we'll talk with you openly about why, help you find a program that fits and coordinate the handoff where we can. Many people return to us for ongoing care afterward. Needing more support is not a failure. It's about getting the right help at the right time.

If you're in immediate danger, call or text 988, call 911 or go to your nearest emergency room. See our crisis resources.
